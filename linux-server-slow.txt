How to Explain the Root Causes (Opening Summary)

"When a Linux server becomes unresponsive despite CPU utilization sitting at 20%, the 
CPU is mostly idle because processes are blocked waiting on non-CPU resources. 
The primary suspects are high Disk I/O wait, severe memory exhaustion causing heavy 
swapping, network or DNS bottlenecks, or stuck processes in uninterruptible sleep (D state)."

Step-by-Step Troubleshooting Flow


1. Check Load Average & CPU Wait States (uptime, top / vmstat)

• Commands: uptime, vmstat 1
• What to look for: Compare the 1, 5, and 15-minute load averages against the number of CPU cores (nproc). In Linux,
 load average includes both runnable processes and processes waiting on uninterruptible I/O.
• Key metric: In top or vmstat 1, check the wa (I/O wait) percentage. If us/sy are low (20%) but wa is high, the CPU is
 sitting idle waiting for slow storage.

2. Check Memory Pressure & Swap Thrashing (free -m)

• Commands: free -m, vmstat 1
• What to look for: Look at the available column (not just free) and check Swap usage.
• Why it matters: If available RAM is near zero and si/so (swap in/swap out) in vmstat are actively climbing, the kernel
 is thrashing—constantly moving pages between slow disk swap and RAM. Also check dmesg -T | grep -i oom to see if the
  Out-Of-Memory killer is terminating processes.

3. Check Disk I/O Saturation & Latency (iostat, iotop)

• Commands: iostat -xz 1, iotop -o
• What to look for:
	• %util: Values near 100% indicate the storage device is saturated.
	• await: High read/write latency in milliseconds means disk requests are queuing up.
	• iotop -o: Pinpoints the exact process generating heavy disk reads or writes.

4. Inspect Processes & D-State Tasks (ps, top)

• Commands:bash
ps aux --sort=-%mem | head -n 10
ps -eo pid,user,stat,%mem,%cpu,cmd --sort=-%cpu | head 
Use code with caution.
• What to look for: Identify runaway memory consumers and check for processes in D state (uninterruptible sleep), 
which typically happens when a process is stuck waiting on disk I/O or a hung NFS/network mount.

5. Check Disk Space & Inode Exhaustion (df)

• Commands: df -h and df -i
• What to look for: A full /var or / partition (100% Use%) stalls logging, databases, and temporary file writes. 
Even if df -h shows free space, df -i checks whether 100% of inodes are used up by millions of tiny files, which 
prevents new files from being created.

6. Bonus SRE Checks (What Sets a Senior Answer Apart)

• Steal Time (%st) on Cloud VMs: In top, check st (steal time). On AWS/cloud instances, high %st means the underlying 
hypervisor is throttling your VM or noisy neighbors are saturating host resources.
• Network & DNS Latency: Run ss -s or netstat -an to check for connection exhaustion, or check /var/log/syslog /
 journalctl -xe for hardware, filesystem, or network errors.
